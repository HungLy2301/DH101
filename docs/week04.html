Make 04 · Week 04 GIF · Remix culture Tool: ChatGPT

Progress Loop

Eight seconds. A bar fills. A bar resets. By the fifth loop, I started noticing the reset more than the progress.

The Artifact

8-second loop. Black and white. Minimal by design.

Your Take

When an image repeats

When an image repeats instead of standing still, it stops being a single moment and becomes a process. In my GIF, the progress bar fills from 0% to 100% and then empties. A still image could show either "empty" or "full," but the loop shows change over time. By the fifth loop, I started noticing not just the progress, but the reset.

Looping changes attention

Looping pulls the eye back to the bar. It shapes meaning: instead of celebrating 100%, the reset questions what "completion" really means. Emotionally, it feels slightly frustrating, because the work disappears and begins again.

What the form remixes

The progress bar has its own remix history. Loading screens, progress indicators, completion bars — they are how software communicates effort to users. By stripping the bar of any context (no app name, no task, no destination), the GIF remixes that familiar form into something uncanny. It borrows the visual grammar of productivity software and empties it of purpose. This connects to a broader tradition of culture jamming, where familiar symbols are recontextualized to expose hidden assumptions. The "loading" metaphor promises arrival. The loop refuses it.

Why this is about labor

In the context of digital labor — where gig workers, data labelers, and content moderators repeat the same task endlessly without accumulation — the loop stops being abstract. The bar filling and resetting is also what it looks like to do work that the system does not remember.

The collaboration

AI helped execute the animation quickly but did not decide the concept or meaning. The looping structure and its interpretation come from my intention. The GIF shows that while AI can automate repetition, humans still define what that repetition signifies.

Weekly reflection · Week 04

Is AI a collaborator, tool, or plagiarist in storytelling?

I think AI can be a collaborator and a tool, but calling it a plagiarist depends on how it is used. As a tool, AI helps generate ideas, outlines, or drafts quickly. As a collaborator, it feels like a partner you can brainstorm with. However, AI does not have original experiences or emotions. It creates stories based on patterns it learned from existing texts. Because of that, there is a risk of repeating or closely echoing other works. I do not see AI itself as intentionally plagiarizing, since it does not have intent. The responsibility falls on the human using it. In storytelling, AI works best as support, while the human remains the true author and decision-maker.
