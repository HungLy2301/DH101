Make 02 · Week 02 Identity · AI image generation Tool: ChatGPT

Two Selves, One Algorithm

Two AI-generated portraits of me — one literal, one symbolic. Both miss the same thing: the difference between describing a person and being one.

The Artifact

First portrait: physical likeness, student context.

Second portrait: values rendered as scenery.

Your Take

Two prompts, two failures

I prompted ChatGPT to generate two images of myself. The first asked for physical accuracy: a 21-year-old Vietnamese international student, glasses, a 7/3 side part, a stylish look. The result is technically credible. It looks roughly like me. It does not, of course, know me.

The second prompt asked the model to incorporate things that matter to me — treating people kindly, loving nature, advocating for equality, wanting to feel fulfilled. The model translated all of this into a single visual move: it put me in a garden and pinned a daisy on my jacket. Love of nature became proximity to plants. The other values were not visible at all.

What AI sees, what it can't

AI has access to enormous pools of visual and textual data and can produce images that resemble specific individuals when the input is detailed enough. What it cannot do is represent internal qualities — values, emotions, lived experience — that are not visually coded. It defaults to the visible stand-ins for those qualities (gardens for nature, smiles for happiness, tidy clothing for professionalism) because those are the patterns most densely represented in its training data.

Authenticity, co-constructed

When identity is co-constructed with AI, authenticity becomes limited. AI builds identities by combining existing data and visual patterns rather than understanding who a person actually is. Even when humans guide the prompt, the result reflects surface-level traits rather than deeper qualities. The identity AI produces is mostly a combination of perceptions — what other people have already documented about people like you — rather than a representation of you.

Attribution & AI Use

Tools used
ChatGPT (image generation feature)
AI prompts
Prompt 1: "Create a selfie of myself according to this description: I'm a 21-year-old Vietnamese international student at a private liberal arts school in the US. I have a squarish face, wear glasses, have a 7/3 side part, relaxing black hair, and a stylish look."

Prompt 2: "Now alter the selfie to factor in these facts about myself: treat people very nice, love nature, want to advocate for equality and peace, be successful in life, and always feel fulfilled about everything I did."
What AI generated
Two photorealistic portrait-style images.
What I changed or decided
The two-prompt structure was deliberately chosen to test what AI does with abstract values. The reflection analyzing the gap between visual likeness and authentic identity is my own writing.
Sources
None external.

Weekly reflection · Week 02

Can AI help us define what it means to be human, or does it reduce us to patterns?

I think AI can do both: it highlights patterns in human behavior, language, and creativity, showing common threads across experiences, but it cannot fully grasp our emotions, values, or intentions. By reflecting our habits and choices, AI can help us see aspects of ourselves more clearly. At the same time, relying on patterns risks reducing the complexity of humanity to data points, overlooking individuality, context, and depth. AI can illuminate certain truths about humans, but it cannot replace the lived, subjective experience that defines what it truly means to be human.
