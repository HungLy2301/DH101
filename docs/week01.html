Make 01 · Week 01 Reverse-engineering · Analysis Subject: DALL·E

The Anatomy of an Image

What is actually happening when DALL·E turns a sentence into a picture? A system-level breakdown of data, labor, and the choices the interface hides.

The Artifact

Project examined:
openai.com/dall-e

Prompt → model → image

Output: Synthetic images from text prompts.
Data: Web-scraped image-text pairs.
Tools: Text-to-image model + ChatGPT guardrails.
Labor: Curation, training, moderation, RLHF.
Argument: The interface makes generation feel playful while hiding the data sources and power structures behind it.

Pipeline diagram: prompt → model → image.

Your Take

What is being made

DALL·E produces synthetic images based on text prompts — illustrations, paintings, posters, realistic scenes that did not previously exist. The interface presents this as a creative tool. The reality is a layered system in which prompts, models, training data, and moderation policies all participate in producing the output.

The data is the argument

DALL·E's training data reflects the biases of its sources: predominantly English-language captions, Western aesthetic conventions, and images that were already on the public web. What is excluded — images behind paywalls, images from communities that don't widely publish online, non-Western artistic traditions, any visual knowledge that was never digitized — gets laundered into outputs that feel neutral but aren't. Early image models consistently underrepresented darker skin tones, non-Western dress, and disability. This is not a single design decision. It is the statistical residue of training.

Human labor & decisions

Humans collected the training data. Humans curated and labeled it. Humans designed the model architecture, set safety limits, and decide what the system is allowed to generate. The machine produces the final image, but people shape the system's boundaries and creative direction.

Design as argument

The interface makes image creation feel easy and playful. That ease is the design's central claim: creativity is democratized, accessible, consequence-free. The ease also conceals the data sources, the labor, the moderation policies, and the power structures that make the output possible. DALL·E doesn't just generate images. It generates a particular relationship to creativity — one in which the conditions of production are invisible by default.

What counts as "making"

In DALL·E, making is not a single act of drawing or designing, but a layered process. It happens when users describe ideas, when ChatGPT reshapes those ideas into prompts, and when the model generates images based on learned patterns. When machines participate, creativity becomes shared between humans and systems. Human values still shape what the machine can create — through training choices, safety rules, and interface design. Making with AI shifts authorship into a collaborative but uneven relationship: the machine produces outputs while humans define the limits, goals, and meanings behind them.

Weekly reflection · Week 01

What counts as "making" in the age of AI?

In the age of AI, "making" extends beyond traditional hands-on creation to include guiding, prompting, and collaborating with machines. Making can involve defining ideas, shaping prompts, and curating outputs, while the AI executes tasks like generating images or text. When machines participate, we gain speed, scale, and access to possibilities that humans alone might not produce. However, we may lose some control over interpretation, meaning, and originality, since AI lacks understanding or intention. Authorship becomes shared and less visible, and the creative process can feel less personal. At the same time, AI opens space for new forms of collaboration, experimentation, and exploration of ideas, highlighting both the potentials and limits of human-machine creativity.
