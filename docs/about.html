About

Who I Am

I'm Hung Ly, a Financial Economics student (Mathematics minor) at Denison University in Granville, Ohio. My academic background is in numbers — financial modeling, econometrics, investment analysis — but my curiosity has always pushed toward the question underneath the numbers: who decides what gets measured, and what gets left out?

DH 101: Critical Making in the Age of AI gave me a language for that question. Through a semester of making — comics, maps, games, infographics, data visualizations, and speculative fiction — I moved from thinking about AI as a tool I use to thinking about it as a system of power I live inside. This portfolio is a record of that shift.

Outside the classroom, I've applied these instincts in real contexts: analyzing economic and demographic data for a $2M+ corridor revitalization initiative at Cleveland Neighborhood Progress, building financial models for 50+ product lines at Denison Consulting, and competing in the L'Oréal Brandstorm and Accenture case competitions. I'm interested in work that sits at the intersection of data, storytelling, and accountability — wherever that leads.

My Approach

I work across methods: financial modeling and econometric analysis on one end; critical mapping, network visualization, and interactive narrative on the other. What ties those practices together is a commitment to making visible what systems tend to hide — whether that's inventory waste in a supply chain, labor conditions behind a content platform, or the water a data center draws from a drought-stressed aquifer.

I use AI deliberately. I treat it as a collaborator for structural thinking and a pressure-tester for arguments — not a ghostwriter. Every piece of work here reflects my own editorial judgment. You can read exactly how I used AI across this semester on the How I Use AI page.
