I'm a Financial Economics student with a quiet preference for the question underneath the numbers: who decides what gets measured, and what gets left out?

My academic background is quantitative — financial modeling, econometrics, risk analysis. My instinct, though, has always pushed sideways: toward the part of the spreadsheet that doesn't fit in a column. Which assumption is carrying the analysis? Whose labor is missing from the model? What does this number conceal that the company chose not to publish?

DH 101: Critical Making in the Age of AI gave me a language for those questions, and a set of tools — counter-mapping, network analysis, speculative design, infographic, interactive narrative — to work with them. The result is this portfolio: a record of moving from thinking about AI as a tool to thinking about it as a system of power.

Outside the classroom, I've put these instincts to work in real contexts. As an Economic Development Intern at Cleveland Neighborhood Progress, I analyzed data for 10+ neighborhoods and built asset maps supporting a $2M+ corridor revitalization. At Denison Consulting, I built financial models for 50+ product lines and ran comparable analyses for 50+ potential partner companies. I've competed in the L'Oréal Brandstorm and Accenture case competitions, and once founded a small e-commerce candle business just to see if I could.

The through-line, I think, is the same instinct that shows up in this portfolio: looking for the structure underneath the surface, and insisting that what gets hidden is part of what's true.
