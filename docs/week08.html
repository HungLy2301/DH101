Your Take

The grammar I built

I built my generator using Tracery, a grammar-based text generation tool. The concept: surreal product descriptions for objects that don't exist. Listings for things that deal in emotion, memory, and dread rather than function. The grammar contains 12 symbols — product_name, material, color, shape, function, side_effect, sensation, origin_story, testimonial, price_phrase, warning, origin. I built three sentence templates so outputs vary structurally, not just lexically.

The tonal target

Each symbol contains 5–8 options. I wrote them with a specific target in mind: unsettling and dreamy, leaning on emotional abstraction rather than concrete imagery. Materials are things like "compressed forgetting" and "woven sleep residue." Functions include "redistributes unexpressed grief." I wanted every field to feel like it belonged to the same emotional vocabulary even when combined randomly.

Sixty outputs, seven selected

I generated approximately 60 outputs by clicking regenerate in the editor and copying results into a running document. Generation was fast, producing a new description instantly with each click. Many outputs were coherent but unremarkable; some were flat. I stopped generating when I had enough variety to curate from meaningfully, and selected 7 from the pool of 60.

Curation as authorship

The creative decisions were not made when I wrote the grammar — or not only then. They were made in the moments I stopped clicking. Curation is its own kind of authorship: slower, more uncomfortable, and more revealing than generation. After about twenty outputs, I stopped being surprised by individual combinations and started evaluating them against each other. I was no longer asking "is this interesting?" but "is this the most interesting version of this idea?"

Where the human is

Where is the human in generative work? I think the honest answer is: everywhere except the middle. I defined the inputs, I selected the outputs, and everything in between was a process I set in motion but did not control. The outputs I chose share something the rejected ones didn't — a feeling of internal coherence, where each random combination somehow confirms the others. That coherence was never programmed. It was noticed. And noticing, I think, is still a human act, for now.

Attribution & AI Use

Tool
Tracery, a free grammar-based text generation tool available at tracery.io.
Grammar authorship
All grammar rules, word lists, and sentence templates were written by me and entered into the Tracery editor as JSON. The grammar contains 12 symbols and 3 output templates.
AI use
No large-language-model AI writing tools were used to generate the final curated outputs. The outputs are the direct product of the Tracery grammar I constructed, randomly combined by the Tracery tool, and selected and annotated by me.
What I changed or decided
Every word in the grammar is mine. The tonal target ("unsettling and dreamy"), the structural variation across three sentence templates, and the curation of 7 outputs from 60 are all my decisions. The reflection on curation-as-authorship is my own.

Weekly reflection · Week 08

What is creativity when AI can generate endlessly? Where is the human in generative work?

Creativity used to mean making something from nothing. Generative AI complicates that because the making happens automatically and without intention. What is left for the human is not creation in the traditional sense but selection and taste. Building the Tracery bot made this clear quickly. I was in control of the rules and vocabulary, but not the output. The tool combined my words in ways I did not plan. Some results were flat and forgettable. Others felt more resonant than anything I would have written on purpose.

The human in generative work lives in the margins. I wrote every word in the grammar, but the machine arranged them. I generated 60 outputs and chose 7. Those choices were mine and they reflected something real about my judgment. So while I was not in control of the output, I was in control of what it meant.
