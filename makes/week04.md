## Reflection
When an image repeats instead of standing still, it stops being a single moment and becomes a process. In my GIF, the progress bar fills from 0% to 100% and then empties. A still image could show either “empty” or “full,” but the loop shows change over time. The repetition makes the motion impossible to ignore. By the fifth loop, I started noticing not just the progress, but the reset. The constant filling and emptying creates a feeling of effort without final completion. It suggests that progress can be temporary or cyclical.
Looping affects attention because the motion keeps pulling the eye back to the bar. It also shapes meaning: instead of celebrating 100%, the reset questions what “completion” really means. Emotionally, it feels slightly frustrating, because the work disappears and begins again.
The human choices in this GIF include the concept (progress as a bar), the decision to make it 8 seconds, the smooth forward-and-back structure, and the minimal black-andwhite design. I chose clarity over decoration to emphasize the idea. The automation appears in the frame generation and percentage increments, which follow a predictable mathematical pattern.
The progress bar as a form has its own remix history. Loading screens, progress indicators, and completion bars are ubiquitous interface elements — they are how software communicates effort to users. By stripping the bar of any context (no app name, no task, no destination), the GIF remixes that familiar form into something uncanny. It borrows the visual grammar of productivity software and empties it of purpose. This connects to a broader tradition of culture jamming, where familiar symbols are recontextualized to expose hidden assumptions. The "loading" metaphor promises arrival. The loop refuses it. In the context of digital labor — where gig workers, data labelers, and content moderators repeat the same task endlessly without accumulation — the loop stops being abstract. The bar filling and resetting is also what it looks like to do work that the system does not remember.
AI complicates authorship because it helped execute the animation quickly, but it did not decide the concept or meaning. The looping structure and interpretation come from my intention. The GIF shows that while AI can automate repetition, humans still define what that repetition signifies.
