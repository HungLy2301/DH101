# Week 6 Reflection

Reflect 6: What do we gain/lose when machines “read” literature for us?

When machines read literature, they change how we look at it. Instead of moving slowly through each sentence, they scan everything at once. They show repetition, patterns, and structure. This can help us notice things we might overlook, like how often a certain word appears or where an idea becomes stronger in the text. But something important is missing. Reading is not just about patterns. It is also about reaction. Sometimes a line makes us pause, remember something personal, or feel uncomfortable. A machine does not pause or feel anything. It processes information, but it does not respond to it. So we gain a clearer view of structure, but we lose the personal side of reading. Machines can organize a text, but they cannot experience it. Meaning still depends on how a human reader connects to the words.
